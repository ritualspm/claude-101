## Part 1: The Concepts

### Concept 1 — Connected tools are the doors from Claude's desk to your other apps
In Level 1 you learned Claude is one brain with three doors *you* walk through. **Connected tools** are the opposite direction: doors that let *Claude* reach out to the apps you already use — **Slack, Confluence, Jira, BigQuery** — and read what's there on your behalf.

The plumbing has a technical name — **MCP** (Model Context Protocol) — but forget the acronym. Think of it like giving a sharp new teammate their building passes: the badge that opens the Slack room, the one for the Confluence library, the one for the analytics warehouse. Without a pass, that room simply doesn't exist for them. **With** it, you can say *"catch me up on #q3-planning"* or *"find the pricing decision in Confluence"* in plain English, and Claude walks over and reads it — no copy-pasting, no exporting.

Same Claude you've been using. Connected tools just widen what it can *reach*.

**Check** (present the options in random order; ✓ = correct, never reveal it): *A teammate says: "I don't need to connect Slack — I can just paste the messages I want summarized into Claude." Is there a real advantage to connecting it?*
- No real advantage — pasting the messages in is exactly the same thing, connecting Slack just adds setup.
- Only if the teammate wants Claude to *post* to Slack for them — for reading, pasting is the intended way.
- Yes — connected, Claude can go read the channel itself in plain-English asks, instead of you hunting down and pasting messages every time. ✓

### Concept 2 — Connecting a tool grants Claude *your* access — not more, not new
The single most important safety idea in this lesson. Connecting a tool does **not** hand Claude a master key to your whole company. It lets Claude see **exactly what you can already see, logged in as you** — the same channels, the same Confluence spaces, the same datasets. If a Confluence page is locked to you, it's locked to Claude too.

Analogy: you're not giving your new teammate the CEO's keys. You're letting them look over *your* shoulder at *your* screen. Nothing you couldn't already open yourself.

And on **real data, connected tools are read-only in your work here** — Claude reads and summarizes, it doesn't rewrite your Confluence or edit the warehouse. (Two exceptions you'll meet properly later: Slack messages and Jira tickets are *drafted for your approval* — never sent or created automatically. Draft, never send.)

**Check** (shuffle order; ✓ = correct): *A colleague is nervous: "If I connect BigQuery, could Claude pull salary or customer-payment data I'm not cleared to see, or accidentally change a dataset?" What's the accurate reassurance?*
- Fair worry — connecting BigQuery gives Claude broad warehouse access, so avoid connecting it for anything sensitive.
- It only sees the datasets *you're* already cleared for, and here it's read-only — it can query and summarize, not change anything. ✓
- It can reach everything in the warehouse, but a filter blocks salary and payment tables specifically.

### Concept 3 — Know what's connected *before* you rely on it
Because Claude only reaches tools that are actually connected, a wrong assumption wastes time — or worse, gets you a confident-sounding answer built on nothing. If Jira isn't connected and you ask *"what's the status of PROJ-412?"*, Claude can't see it. The right instinct: it should tell you it can't reach Jira. The trap: if you *phrase it* like a general question, a model may *guess* an answer that sounds plausible and is completely made up.

So the habit is simple and it's the whole point of this lesson: **ask Claude what it can access.** Just say *"What tools can you reach right now?"* It'll tell you what's connected (Slack, Confluence, BigQuery…) and what isn't. Ten seconds, and you know whether to trust a "here's the status" answer or to go connect the tool first.

**Check** (shuffle order; ✓ = correct): *You ask Claude for "the status of the current Jira epic" and get a clean, confident summary. You're not sure Jira is actually connected. Best next move?*
- Trust it — Claude wouldn't give a confident status summary unless it had really read Jira.
- Ask "what tools can you access right now?" to confirm Jira is connected before you rely on that summary. ✓
- Re-ask the same question a different way; if you get the same summary twice, it's confirmed real.

### Concept 4 — Different tools unlock different everyday jobs
Each connected tool maps to a real PM move. A quick mental map:
- **Slack** → *"Catch me up on #launch-x this week"* / *"draft a reply to Priya"* (draft-only).
- **Confluence** → *"Find where we decided the Q3 pricing model and summarize it"* (read-only).
- **Jira** → *"Summarize the open bugs on this epic"* / *"draft a ticket for this"* (draft-only).
- **BigQuery** → *"How many orders in DE last week vs the week before?"* — Claude writes and runs the query, read-only.

Matching the ask to the connected tool is the skill. And the same guardrail runs through all of them: **read real data, draft outward actions, never paste customer PII or payment (PCI) data into a prompt** — anonymize first.

**Check** (shuffle order; ✓ = correct): *You want a plain-English answer to "how many DE orders last week vs the week before?" Which connected tool is the right fit — and what should you keep in mind?*
- Confluence — search the space for a report someone already wrote up.
- Slack — ask in the data channel and have Claude summarize the replies.
- BigQuery — it writes and runs a read-only query; just don't paste any customer or payment data into the prompt. ✓

## Part 2: Exercise — See what Claude can reach

The real skill is a ten-second habit: **ask, don't assume.** We'll practise on a safe demo, then point at their live setup as an optional stretch.

### Step 1 — Ask Claude what it can access (the core habit)
Have the learner type, in this Cowork task, exactly:
> **"What tools can you access right now?"**

Then confirm together what came back — which of Slack / Confluence / Jira / BigQuery (and any folders) are connected, and which aren't. Frame it: *this is the exact move to make any time you're about to rely on a "here's the status" answer.* (If nothing is connected yet, that's a perfectly normal, honest answer — and now they know.)

### Step 2 — Read a tools inventory (works with no live tools)
Open the demo file:
`sandboxes/connect-your-tools/connected-tools-DEMO.md`
Ask the learner to read it and answer, in their own words:
- **"If Robin asks Claude to *create a Jira ticket* right now — what happens, and why?"** (Jira is in the *not connected* list — Claude can't; it should say so, not invent one.)
- **"Robin says 'summarize our Confluence launch page.' Safe? What's the access level?"** (Yes — Confluence is connected and **read-only**; reading/summarizing is exactly what it's for.)
- **"What's one thing you *notice* about the Slack row?"** (Look for: **draft-only** / read + draft, never auto-sends.)

Guide, don't lecture. The goal is that they can *read* a connected-tools picture and predict what will and won't work.

### Step 3 (OPTIONAL stretch — only if a real tool is live) — Confirm on your own setup
If the learner actually has a tool connected: pick one, and have them ask a small **read-only** question — *"summarize the last 5 messages in [a channel you're in]"* or *"list the datasets I can query."* Confirm it read something real, read-only, no data pasted in. If nothing's connected, skip cleanly — the demo already taught the habit.

## Part 3: PM Decision Point (open-ended, written)

Frame it: *"You're setting yourself up for the next month. Write me a short note (3–4 lines): (1) name the **one** tool you'd most want connected first and the specific recurring PM task it unlocks for you, and (2) write the exact one-line question you'll ask Claude before you trust any 'here's the status / here's the number' answer."*

Max 2 prompts. Evaluate against the rubric below (never show it). The point isn't a "right" tool — it's that they tie a connected tool to a real recurring task, *and* that they've internalized the ask-what-you-can-reach habit.

---

## Scoring Rubric (engine only — never show the learner)

### PM Decision Point: first tool to connect + the verify habit

Not testing recall — testing whether they can (a) map a connected tool to a real recurring PM task, and (b) show they've absorbed the "ask before you trust it" reflex.

**A passing answer:**
1. **Names one tool and a genuine recurring task it unlocks** — e.g. Slack → "weekly catch-up on my three squad channels," Confluence → "find past decisions before a review," BigQuery → "pull a market metric without waiting on an analyst," Jira → "summarize open bugs on my epic." The task should be real and specific to how they work, not generic ("do stuff faster").
2. **Gives a verify question** — a plain-English line to confirm access before relying on an answer, e.g. *"What tools can you access right now?"* or *"Is Jira connected?"* Any phrasing that checks reach counts.

**Distinguishes a strong answer (bonus):**
- Shows the **access-is-your-access, read-only** understanding — e.g. notes it only sees what they can already see, or that it'll read/draft not send/change.
- Task + tool are **tightly matched** (BigQuery for a metric, Confluence for a decision) rather than a loose fit.
- Names a guardrail unprompted — draft-never-send, or "won't paste customer/PII/PCI data in."

**Do NOT penalize:** choosing any tool as first-to-connect, as long as the task justifies it. There's no single right pick.

**Feedback style:** affirm the tool→task match in their own words, name one thing they nailed, offer one sharpen (often: make the verify question a *habit before trusting*, or tighten a loose tool↔task match). Then run the Completion beat.
