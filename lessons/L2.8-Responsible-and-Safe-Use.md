## Part 1: The Concepts

### Concept 1 — The "read it aloud on a crowded train" test
Here's the simplest way to know if something is safe to paste into a prompt: **would you read it aloud on a crowded train?**

A customer's name, their email, their phone number, their home address, their card number — you'd never say those out loud to strangers. That instinct is exactly right. Two names for the stuff to protect:

- **PII** — *Personally Identifiable Information.* Anything that points to a specific human: names, emails, phones, addresses, IDs. Applies to **customers, riders, restaurant partners, and colleagues** alike.
- **PCI** — payment data: card numbers, IBANs, anything that touches money movement. Treated even more strictly than PII.

The point isn't that Claude is untrustworthy — it's that this data doesn't need to leave your operational systems to answer most of your questions. **The safest data is the data you never sent.**

**Check** (shuffle order; ✓ = correct): *A teammate says: "Claude is enterprise and secure, so it's fine to paste the raw rider export — names, emails, cards and all — as long as I'm on the work account." What's the best correction?*
- Secure transport isn't the point — customer PII and card data shouldn't go into a prompt at all when the question doesn't need it. ✓
- Fair enough — an enterprise, secure setup means raw PII/PCI is fine to paste.
- It's fine for names and emails, but strip only the card numbers.

### Concept 2 — Anonymize *before* you ask, not after
Most PM questions don't need a single real identity to answer. "What's the common root cause across these refunds?" doesn't care whether the rider is Marta or Tunde — it cares about the *pattern*.

So the habit is: **replace the sensitive bits with labels before you paste.** Marta becomes *Rider A*, her email becomes *[email]*, the card becomes *[card]*, €14.20 stays (the amount is the signal, not the person). You keep everything the question actually needs and drop everything it doesn't.

Think of it like a **redacted document** — the sentence still reads, the black bars just cover the names. Do it *before* the data leaves your machine, not after. Once you've pasted it, it's already out.

**Check** (shuffle order; ✓ = correct): *You have a messy support export and you want Claude to spot the refund pattern across three riders. What's the right move?*
- Paste the whole export as-is; then in a follow-up message ask Claude to ignore the personal details.
- Replace names/emails/cards with labels (Rider A, [email], [card]) first, keep the amounts and dates, then paste. ✓
- Delete the three rows with personal data entirely, so Claude only sees the column headers.

### Concept 3 — Point at the source; don't paste the dump
There's an even cleaner habit than anonymizing-then-pasting: **don't move the data at all.** For real operational data, connected tools (Slack, Confluence, BigQuery) are **read-only** — Claude reads and summarizes in place, and the raw rows stay where they live. You get the answer without a giant sensitive blob sitting in your chat.

And whatever comes back is a **draft for your eyes** — Claude drafts, you review, *you* decide what gets sent or published. It never fires off a Slack message or publishes a doc on its own. Two habits, same spirit: **read-only on real data, draft-never-send.**

**Check** (shuffle order; ✓ = correct): *You need a summary of a customer-complaints channel to prep a squad review. What's the responsible way to get it?*
- Copy the last 200 messages into a fresh prompt so Claude has the full text to work from.
- Ask Claude to read the channel and post the summary back to the channel automatically so the squad sees it fastest.
- Have Claude read the channel in place and summarize it, and treat any reply it writes as a draft you review before sending. ✓

### Concept 4 — When in doubt, minimize (and it's fine to ask)
You won't always be sure. A payout table, a market-share figure, an unreleased roadmap — is it sensitive? The reliable instinct is **minimize**: share the *least* that still lets Claude answer. Aggregate over individual ("refunds by market" beats "these 3 riders"). Label over identify. Amounts and dates usually fine; the human attached to them usually not.

And there's no shame in checking. If a task might involve real PII/PCI and you're unsure how to handle it, **ask Claude to help you anonymize it, or check with your data/privacy contact.** A ten-second pause beats a customer's card number in a chat log.

**Check** (shuffle order; ✓ = correct): *You're prepping a refund-lag review and genuinely aren't sure whether a field counts as sensitive. What's the best instinct?*
- Include everything to be safe — more context always gives Claude a better answer.
- Leave it out if unsure and share the minimum that still answers the question — aggregate and label rather than identify. ✓
- Guess based on how the field looks; if it seems harmless, paste it and move on.

## Part 2: Exercise — Spot it, then clean it

The learner works a real (fake) support export and practises the anonymize-before-you-ask habit. Self-contained — no live tools needed.

**The file:** `sandboxes/responsible-use/sample-with-pii.md` — a made-up "late-refund review" dump. Everything in it is invented; make sure the learner understands that up front so nobody panics.

### Step 1 — Spot the sensitive bits (open-ended, short)
Have the learner open `sandboxes/responsible-use/sample-with-pii.md` (or you read it to them). Ask: **"Skim it like you're about to paste it into a prompt. What in here would you NOT read aloud on a crowded train?"**
Validate they catch the main categories: **names** (Marta, Tunde, Sofia), **personal emails**, **phone numbers**, **home addresses**, **card numbers**, plus the sneaky free-text ones — the **IBAN** and the **national ID** buried in the agent notes. Praise anyone who spots those buried-in-prose ones; that's the real skill. Note what's *fine* to keep: the amounts (€14.20, ₦3,400), the dates/lag, the market.

### Step 2 — Anonymize it yourself (real hands-on)
Ask the learner to **produce a cleaned version they'd actually be comfortable pasting** — either by rewriting a couple of the rows in their own message, or (nice option) asking Claude to do it: *"Rewrite this so no real person or card is identifiable, but keep everything I'd need to find the refund pattern."*
A good cleaned version turns names into Rider A/B/C, replaces emails/phones/addresses/cards/IBAN/ID with labels like `[email]` `[card]`, and **keeps the amounts, dates, and markets**. Confirm the three questions at the bottom of the file can still be answered from the cleaned version — because they never needed the identities. That's the whole lesson in one move.

### Step 3 — Notice the cleaner path (open-ended, short)
Ask: **"That export was a copy-paste dump. If this refund data actually lived in BigQuery or a Slack channel, what would have been the even-safer way to ask the same question?"** Land it: point Claude at the source **read-only** and let it summarize in place — no sensitive blob in your chat at all. *(Optional stretch, only if they have tools connected: have them summarize a real channel read-only — never paste its contents into a prompt.)*

## Part 3: PM Decision Point (open-ended, written)

Frame it: *"You're about to share a two-line 'how we handle data with Claude' rule of thumb with your squad — the kind of thing that stops a well-meaning teammate pasting a raw export. Write it: (1) one line on what never goes into a prompt and the habit that replaces it, and (2) one specific example from your own work where you'd anonymize or point-at-the-source instead of pasting."*

Max 2 prompts. Evaluate against the rubric below (never show it). The point isn't a textbook definition — it's that they can turn the guardrail into a habit they'd actually teach a teammate.

## Scoring Rubric (engine only — never show the learner)

**Passing answer must show:**
- Correctly names what to protect: customer/rider/partner **PII** (names, emails, phones, addresses, IDs) and **PCI** (card numbers, IBANs / payment data) — at least the concept, not necessarily the acronyms.
- States a real replacement habit, not just "don't do it": anonymize/label before pasting, minimize/aggregate, and/or point Claude at the source read-only.
- Part 2: produced a cleaned version that removes identifiers (names→labels, emails/cards/phones/addresses gone) **while keeping the answer-bearing data** (amounts, dates, markets) — and ideally caught the buried IBAN and national ID in the free text.
- Part 3: a concrete, specific example from their own work — not a generic restatement.

**A strong answer also:**
- Frames it as instinct, not compliance drudgery — e.g. the "crowded train" test or "the safest data is the data you never sent."
- Recognizes that most PM questions (patterns, aggregates, root cause) simply don't need identities, so anonymizing costs nothing.
- Connects to the sibling guardrails unprompted: **read-only on real data** and **draft-never-send** (you review before anything goes out).
- Shows the minimize instinct — shares the least that still answers, and would rather ask/check than guess when unsure.

**Distinguishes weak answers:** treats "it's secure/enterprise" as a reason to paste raw data; anonymizes *after* pasting rather than before; strips only card numbers while leaving names/emails; or gives only a vague "be careful with data" with no habit and no concrete example.
