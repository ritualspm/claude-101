# L2.5 — Your Home Base (`~/.claude`)

**Course:** Using Claude · **Level 2 — Make It Yours** | ~22 min
**Part times:** Concepts ~11 min | Exercise ~7 min | Decision Point ~4 min
**Previous lesson:** L2.4 — Memory (stop re-explaining yourself; facts Claude remembers about you).

**WHO THE LEARNER IS (load-bearing):** A product person, likely brand new to Claude. Teach through analogy and their own screen. They're most likely in Cowork right now — use that. **Never show code or config.** `~/.claude` is a folder path — say it once, then always call it the **"home base"** or **"personal desk drawer."** The whole point of this lesson is *reassurance and a mental map*, not file-editing — nobody should ever hand-edit these files; the everyday move is always to **ask Claude.** Anchor everything to real PM work (their rules, their memory, their saved shortcuts).

**HOW TO RUN THE CHECKS:** Each concept ends with a multiple-choice check — clickable cards (`AskUserQuestion`) if available, else A/B/C. Shuffle the option order with the random command before presenting; correct option marked ✓ (never shown). One question per turn; always explain the outcome before teaching the next concept.

## Part 1: The Concepts

### Concept 1 — There's one place all your Claude setup lives (your desk drawer)
Over the last few lessons you've been *making Claude yours* — a rulebook (L2.3), memory about you (L2.4). Natural question: **where does all that actually go?**

It goes to one tidy spot on your computer called your **home base** (the technical name is `~/.claude` — say it once, then forget it). Think of it as your **personal desk drawer**: your rulebook, your notebook of remembered facts, your saved shortcuts, your quiet preferences — all in one drawer, all belonging to *you*.

You almost never open the drawer yourself. Just like you don't reach into a filing cabinet to file a memo — you hand it to your assistant and *they* file it. The everyday move is always to **ask Claude** ("what do you remember about me?", "save this as a rule") and it puts things in the right place. Knowing the drawer *exists* is what stops it feeling like magic.

**Check** (present the options in random order; ✓ = correct, never reveal it): *A teammate says: "So my rules and memory are floating somewhere in the cloud, and to change them I'd have to go find and edit the files myself." What's the best correction?*
- Right — they're stored remotely, so editing the underlying files by hand is the way to change them.
- They live in one folder on your own computer — your home base — and you change them by *asking Claude*, not by editing files yourself. ✓
- They're baked into the app and can't really be changed once set — you'd start a fresh setup instead.

### Concept 2 — What's actually in the drawer (four kinds of thing)
You don't need the file names — you need to recognise the *four kinds of thing* that live in your home base, because they map exactly to features you've already met or will meet:

- **Your rulebook** — how you like Claude to behave (tone, "always draft, never send"). *(L2.3)*
- **Your memory** — the notebook of facts about you and your team. *(L2.4)*
- **Your skills** — saved one-command workflows, like a `/weekly` review. *(coming in L3.1)*
- **Your agents & settings** — saved personas you can consult, plus quiet preferences like *default model = Sonnet*. *(agents in L3.3)*

That's the whole drawer. When someone says "it's all in your `~/.claude`," this is what they mean — four familiar things in one place.

**Check** (shuffle order; ✓ = correct): *You've saved a `/weekly` shortcut, taught Claude your tone, and it remembers your squad. A colleague asks "where does all that stuff live?" Which answer is right?*
- In three separate apps — the shortcut, the tone, and the memory are each stored by a different feature.
- All in your one home base drawer — your skills, rulebook, and memory are different *kinds of thing* in the same place. ✓
- Only the tone is saved anywhere; shortcuts and memory are rebuilt fresh each session.

### Concept 3 — Global vs per-folder: your drawer vs a labelled project box
This is the one distinction worth really getting. There are **two levels** where your setup can live:

- **Global** — your home base drawer. This is **"about me, always true"**: your tone, your team, "always draft." It follows you into *every* project and through *every* door (Chat / Cowork / Code). Set it once, it's everywhere.
- **Per-folder** — a rulebook that lives *inside one project folder*. This is **"about this one initiative"**: e.g. "this launch is DACH-only, the KPI is repeat rate." It only applies when you're working in *that* folder — like a note taped inside one labelled box.

They don't fight — the per-folder rules **layer on top of** the global ones. Global sets who you are; per-folder adds what's true for *this* project.

**Check** (shuffle order; ✓ = correct): *You want Claude to always default to bullet-point summaries — in every project, forever — AND, only for your Q3 launch project, to always treat the scope as DACH-only. Where does each rule belong?*
- Bullets → global (about you, everywhere); DACH-only → in the Q3 project's own rulebook (about that one project). ✓
- Both belong in the global home base — putting anything in a project folder risks it getting lost.
- Both belong in the Q3 project folder — that's the folder you're working in, so that's where rules go.

### Concept 4 — You never hand-edit it — you ask (and it's yours, on your machine)
Two things that make the home base safe and easy to live with:

- **You drive it by asking, not editing.** Want to see what's in the drawer? Ask *"what do you remember about me?"* or *"what skills do I have?"* Want to change something? *"Add a rule that I prefer metric names spelled out."* Claude reads and files it correctly. Hand-editing is for the rare power user — not the everyday move, and not something to worry about.
- **It's yours, on your machine.** Your home base is your personal setup, not a shared company drive. That's exactly why the guardrails still hold: no customer PII or payment (PCI) data belongs in your rules or memory, real data stays read-only, and outward actions stay draft-never-send. A personal drawer is still not the place for sensitive operational data.

**Check** (shuffle order; ✓ = correct): *You want to check what facts Claude currently remembers about you, then add one. What's the right way to do it?*
- Open the memory file in a text editor, read it, and type the new fact in by hand.
- Just ask — "what do you remember about me?" then "add that I prefer bullet-point summaries" — and let Claude file it. ✓
- Start a brand-new setup so the new fact is captured cleanly from scratch.

## Part 2: Exercise — A tour of the drawer (light exploration)

No editing, no risk — just learn to *read* a home-base picture and tell global from per-folder. We practise on a safe demo, then peek at their real setup as an optional stretch.

### Step 1 — Read the map (works with no live tools)
Open the demo file:
`sandboxes/your-home-base/home-base-map-DEMO.md`
It's a pretend home base for a fictional PM, "Robin Example." Ask the learner to read it and answer, in their own words:
- **"Name two of the four kinds of thing sitting in Robin's global drawer."** (Any two of: rulebook, memory, skills, agents/settings.)
- **"Robin's Q3 launch project has its *own* rulebook saying 'DACH-only.' Why isn't that in the global drawer instead?"** (Because it's true only for *that one project* — per-folder = about this initiative; global = about Robin always.)
- **"What's one thing you *notice* about how Robin is meant to change any of this?"** (Look for: you *ask Claude*, you don't hand-edit the files.)

Guide, don't lecture. The goal is that they can *read* a home-base picture and place a rule at the right level.

### Step 2 — Ask about your own drawer (the real habit)
Have the learner type, in this Cowork task, exactly:
> **"What do you currently remember about me, and what skills do I have?"**

Then look at what comes back together. If it's sparse or empty, that's a perfectly honest answer — most people's drawer is nearly bare early on, and that's fine. Frame it: *this ask — not opening any file — is how you'll ever inspect your home base.* (Bonus follow-up they can try: *"where would a rule that only applies to this project go?"* — a nice way to hear the global-vs-per-folder idea in Claude's own words.)

### Step 3 (OPTIONAL stretch — only if they want to) — Feel the two levels
If the learner has a real project folder open in Cowork, have them ask a **read-only** question: *"Does this project have its own rulebook, and what's in my global one?"* Just observe the two levels side by side — global drawer vs this-folder note. No edits. If they've no project folder yet, skip cleanly — the demo already taught the map.

## Part 3: PM Decision Point (open-ended, written)

Frame it: *"You're organising your own Claude setup. Write me a short note (3–4 lines): (1) one rule or fact you'd put in your **global** home base because it's true about you in every project, and (2) one rule you'd keep **per-folder** because it's only true for one specific initiative you work on — name the initiative."*

Max 2 prompts. Evaluate against the rubric below (never show it). The point isn't a "right" rule — it's that they can correctly *sort* a global "about me" rule from a per-folder "about this project" rule, tied to their real work.

---

## Scoring Rubric (engine only — never show the learner)

### PM Decision Point: sorting a global rule from a per-folder rule

Not testing recall of folder names — testing whether they can correctly place a rule at the right *level*: global ("about me, always true") vs per-folder ("about this one initiative").

**A passing answer:**
1. **Gives a genuine global rule/fact** — something true about *them* across every project: tone/format preference ("always bullet points"), their squad/market, timezone, a working habit, "always draft never send." The test is that it would sensibly apply everywhere, not just one project.
2. **Gives a genuine per-folder rule tied to a named initiative** — something true only for one specific project: "for the DACH grocery launch, scope is DACH-only," "for Project Falcon the north-star KPI is repeat rate." It names (or clearly points at) a specific initiative and the rule is scoped to it.

**Distinguishes a strong answer (bonus):**
- The two rules are **cleanly sorted** — the global one really would apply everywhere; the per-folder one really is initiative-specific. No overlap or mis-placement.
- Shows they understand **layering** — per-folder adds *on top of* global, doesn't replace it.
- Names the **ask-don't-edit** instinct, or a guardrail unprompted (no PII/PCI in rules/memory; draft-never-send).

**Do NOT penalize:** any specific rule choice, or being early-career with a thin real setup — a plausible hypothetical rule is fine. There's no single right rule.

**Common miss to catch gently:** putting a clearly project-specific rule in global ("always assume DACH-only" — that would wrongly bleed into every other project), or a clearly universal preference into one folder. If they mis-sort, name it warmly and have them swap the level.

**Feedback style:** affirm the correct sort in their own words, name one thing they nailed, offer one sharpen (often: tighten a rule that's placed at the wrong level, or add the "ask, don't edit" reflex). Then run the Completion beat.
