# Weak prompt (demo asset — FAKE, for training only)

This is the kind of vague, one-line request people fire off on a busy day.
The lesson uses it to practice sharpening a prompt live.

---

## The prompt as typed

> write something about the checkout drop-off thing for the review

---

## Context the person actually had in their head (but never told Claude)

- **Who they are:** a PM on the QuickMart grocery team (a made-up brand).
- **What "the checkout drop-off thing" means:** in the last 2 weeks, users who add items to cart are abandoning at the payment step more than usual.
- **What "something" should be:** a short written update, not a deck.
- **Who "the review" is:** the Monday leadership review — their VP and two peer PMs.
- **What they secretly want:** a crisp problem statement, what we know so far,
  what we don't know yet, and a recommended next step. Skimmable in 60 seconds.
- **Tone:** confident but honest about the gaps. No hand-waving.
- **Length:** about half a page.

None of that is in the prompt. Claude has to guess all of it — so it will
produce something generic, wrongly-scoped, or the wrong format entirely.

---

## Fake supporting numbers (obviously invented — do not treat as real data)

- Cart-to-payment drop-off: ~22% two weeks ago → ~31% last week (made up).
- Spike lines up with a new address-entry screen that shipped on the 24th.
- No confirmed root cause yet; the payments team hasn't been looped in.

(These are placeholders so the exercise has something concrete to work with.
There is no real QuickMart, no real users, no real numbers here.)
